# Course Conclusion

I hope you've enjoyed your time learning the modern foundations of geospatial computing.

This class has aimed to provde a "menu" of geospatial skills: basic building blocks that can be combined together to achieve more complex geospatial tasks. However, as you might have found, **it’s hard to become proficient in Python through a taught course alone!** The best lessons come from trying to take what you have learned and apply it in new contexts and for different goals. My philosophy when designing this course was to try and give you as many opportunities as possible - be it through the homework or the assessed coursework - to be able to explore your own data and workflows, and make your own mistakes and discoveries. Doing your own work, and achieving your own goals, is the ultimate way you will become confident and proficient in geospatial Python.

When you do have a goal in mind - whether through upcoming courses, your final MSc dissertation, or your future career, I hope you now have the skills to achieve it:

 - You know the fundamental scientific and geospatial Python packages you should be using and expanding out from.
 - You know how to access, load, and manipulate geospatial data within Python.
 - You have experiencing of learning new tools and seeing how they integrate into your existing packages and workflows.
 - You know how to find more information! (online searching, documentation, open education content, LLMs, etc.)

More generally, I hope this final week has shown how the basic building blocks we have covered (`numpy`, `pandas`, `geopandas`, `xarray`, `rioxarray`, and friends) are used as the foundation for much more complex geospatial analyses - and has provided a taster for where you might develop your skills in the future. Whatever direction you take your geospatial work in, you'll find that these same tools are at its heart!
